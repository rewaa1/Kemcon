# Phase 2C — Search Intent & Keyword Strategy

**Status:** Research only. No application code, metadata, sitemap, robots or URLs were changed.
**Date of research:** September 2026
**Scope:** Egypt (primary market), English + Arabic treated as separate search ecosystems.

## About the evidence in this document

Two things must be stated plainly before anything else.

**No search volume figures appear in this report.** No keyword-volume tool (Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, Semrush, DataForSEO) was available during this phase. Every volume claim is therefore recorded as **"Search volume not verified."** Where this document ranks a keyword as high or low priority, that ranking is derived from *SERP composition, competitor behaviour and commercial fit* — not from traffic estimates. Anyone reading this should treat priority as "how well this matches Kemcon's business and how winnable it looks", not "how much traffic this will bring".

**SERP observation was geo-limited.** The available search tool is US-geolocated. It surfaces Egyptian sites and Egyptian-language results reliably, but it does **not** reproduce what a user physically in Cairo sees — no local pack, no Google Business Profile results, no Egypt-specific ranking order. Statements about *which sites exist and what language they use* are **verified**. Statements about *ranking positions* are **inferred** and labelled as such.

Every finding below is tagged:

- **[Verified]** — directly observed in a fetched page or search result.
- **[Inferred]** — a reasonable conclusion drawn from observed evidence.
- **[Unverified]** — plausible but not confirmed; needs a proper tool before acting on it.

# 1. Executive Summary

**1. Kemcon has effectively no search presence today. [Verified]**
A search combining the brand name with its own distinguishing details — Kemcon, Egytex, the founder's name, "hotel supplier" — returned nothing about Kemcon at all. Competitors and unrelated Egyptian textile firms filled every result. Before any keyword strategy can pay off, the entity itself has to become findable. This is the single most important finding in this report.

**2. "Curtains" splits into two completely different markets, and Kemcon belongs to only one of them. [Verified]**
The consumer curtain SERP in Egypt is owned by marketplaces — Noon, Jumia, Homzmart, Home Centre — selling ready-made panels with prices in EGP, cash on delivery and free returns. The hospitality/contract SERP is entirely different: service pages, manufacturers, FF&E suppliers, no marketplaces at all. Kemcon is a project company. Chasing the first market means competing with Jumia on a product Kemcon does not sell.

**3. The Arabic hotel-curtain space is genuinely weakly defended. [Verified]**
Searching `ستائر فنادق` and related B2B phrasings surfaced Facebook pages, Saudi retailers, and one article about importing hotel curtain fabric from China. No strong Egyptian hotel-curtain *service page* appeared. Compare that to the consumer side, where 1,334 companies are listed under the Yellow Pages category alone. This is the clearest opening in the entire research.

**4. Egyptian Arabic curtain vocabulary is mostly blinds vocabulary, and Kemcon does not make blinds. [Verified]**
The dominant Egyptian terms — `شيش`, `رول`, `فينيسيان`, `رأسي`, `ويفي`, `زيبرا`, `سن سكرين` — describe roller, venetian, vertical, zebra and screen blinds. The repository is unambiguous: Kemcon makes layered fabric curtains (sheer/voile, drapery, blackout lining) with manual or motorised tracks. There are no blinds anywhere in the product data. A large share of Arabic curtain search demand is therefore *structurally unavailable* to Kemcon and should be treated as a negative keyword set, not a gap.

**5. `تفصيل` is the word that matters in Arabic, not `مخصص`. [Verified]**
Egyptian sites selling made-to-measure curtains use `تفصيل ستائر` almost universally. Kemcon's Arabic pages currently use `مخصصة` (custom/bespoke). Both are correct Arabic; only one is what Egyptians type. This is the highest-value, lowest-risk Arabic finding.

**6. `تنجيد` is the wrong word for the sofa service. [Verified]**
The Egyptian `تنجيد انتريهات` SERP is re-upholstery and repair — reviving a customer's existing worn furniture, sold on price ("أرخص تكلفة", "أسعار المصنع"). Kemcon builds new sofas from the frame up. Targeting this term would attract the wrong enquiry at the wrong price point, and much of that SERP is Saudi rather than Egyptian anyway.

**7. Hospitality in Egypt is not Cairo-only. [Verified]**
A competitor targeting Egyptian hospitality names "Cairo, Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh". Kemcon's own client list includes Red Sea and resort properties. Optimising purely for Cairo would under-serve the resort corridor, where hotel refurbishment cycles are concentrated.

**8. There is a live entity-collision risk on "Egytex". [Verified]**
Kemcon's About page describes "Egytex, the fabrics manufacturing arm of the Group". A search for it returns `egytex.com` — an unrelated "Egypt Official Textile Industry Portal". Leaning on this sub-brand for entity clarity will attach Kemcon to someone else's name.

**9. The industry's own B2B vocabulary is absent from the site. [Verified]**
Competitor and international hospitality suppliers consistently use *FF&E*, *contract textiles*, *soft furnishings*, *custom window treatments*, *hospitality fabrics*. Kemcon's pages use none of these. A procurement manager or an AI system trying to classify Kemcon has no standard industry vocabulary to latch onto.

**10. Very few new pages are justified. Two, at most three. [Inferred]**
The Phase 2A/2B work already produced service pages with correct `Service` schema, real content and clean internal linking. The gap is not page count — it is that no page speaks specifically to the hospitality buyer, who is the stated priority audience.

# 2. Kemcon Search Landscape

## 2.1 What the market actually looks like

Egyptian curtain-related search resolves into four distinct SERP types, and they barely overlap.

| SERP type | Who ranks | Example queries | Kemcon fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marketplace / ecommerce** | Noon, Jumia, Homzmart, Home Centre | `blackout curtains Egypt`, `ستائر بلاك اوت جاهزة` | **None.** Wrong business model |
| **Local directory** | YellowPages.com.eg, EgyDir, EgyCompanies | `شركة ستائر`, `ستائر وشيش القاهرة` | Weak. Listing-worthy, not rankable |
| **Local retail / blinds installer** | Venecia, Sultan Curtains, Sedra, Decurtains, Shutter Flex | `تفصيل ستائر القاهرة`, `ستائر رول` | Partial. Blinds terms excluded |
| **B2B / contract / FF&E** | ElKafoury, WTB, KTC Hospitality, Anglome, Culp, Venus | `hotel curtain supplier`, `hospitality textiles`, `ستائر فنادق` | **Strong. This is Kemcon's market** |

The strategic consequence is simple and worth stating bluntly: **Kemcon should compete almost exclusively in the fourth row.** Its own factory, its named hotel client list, its in-house architect and its installation capability are advantages that mean nothing in a marketplace SERP and mean a great deal in a contract SERP.

## 2.2 Who is already there

**[Verified] Direct or near-direct competitors observed:**

- **ElKafoury Group** — the most instructive competitor found. Operates dedicated B2B landing pages at `/hotel-curtain-supplier/` and `/hotel-ffe-supplier/`, titled *"Hotel Curtain Supplier for Hospitality Projects | 2026"*. Covers Egypt (New Cairo), Saudi Arabia, Oman and Dubai. Their page names blackout fabrics, sheer curtains, decorative drapery, fire-retardant fabrics, and breaks the offer down by hospitality area — guest rooms, executive suites, lobbies, restaurants, conference rooms.
- **Venecia** — described in Egyptian sources as the best-known curtain manufacturer in Egypt; official Somfy agent (motorisation). Produces home, office and hospital curtains. Runs informational content.
- **Cartela Fabrics** — Egyptian fabric manufacturer, ~15 years, "Made in Egypt" positioning.
- **Shutter Flex** — explicitly project-oriented: hospitals, banks, airports, plus residential/industrial/educational.
- **KTC Hospitality** — Egyptian hospitality linen supplier since 1991. The main competitor for the bed-covers cluster.
- **WTB** — Turkish FF&E supplier actively targeting Egyptian hospitality in Cairo, Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh.
- **Anglome (AME)** — luxury bed linen and bathroom textiles for hospitality.

**What this tells us [Inferred]:** the Egyptian hospitality textile market is contested by (a) local curtain retailers moving upmarket, (b) foreign FF&E suppliers moving in, and (c) linen specialists. Nobody observed occupies precisely Kemcon's position: an Egyptian manufacturer that owns its factory, covers curtains *and* seating *and* bedding, employs its own architect, and installs on site. That combination is the differentiator worth building the entity around.

# 6. English vs Arabic — they are not the same market

This section exists because translating the English keyword set into Arabic would produce a strategy that fails.

**Different vocabulary for the same service. [Verified]**
English "custom / bespoke / made-to-measure curtains" maps to Egyptian Arabic **`تفصيل ستائر`** — literally "curtain tailoring". Kemcon's Arabic pages currently say `ستائر مخصصة`. That is good formal Arabic and it matches the schema, but `تفصيل` is the word Egyptian businesses put in their titles and that Egyptian users type. Both can coexist; `تفصيل` should appear in body copy at minimum.

**Different intent at the same query. [Verified]**
`blackout curtains` in English returns marketplaces. `ستائر بلاك اوت` returns a mix of marketplaces, Saudi retailers, catalogue/idea articles and blinds companies — more informational and inspiration-led. Neither is a good Kemcon target, but they fail for different reasons.

**Arabic borrows English wholesale for technical terms. [Verified]**
`بلاك اوت` (blackout), `رول` (roller), `فينيسيان` (venetian), `سن سكرين` (sunscreen), `زيبرا` (zebra) are transliterations. Users mix scripts freely. Kemcon's Arabic copy should not avoid `بلاك اوت` for the sake of purism — it is the real term.

**Arabic B2B is less contested than Arabic B2C. [Verified]**
The consumer Arabic curtain SERP is saturated (1,334 Yellow Pages listings, dozens of retailers). The Arabic *hotel* curtain SERP surfaced Facebook pages and Saudi sites. The English B2B SERP, by contrast, already has a serious competitor with purpose-built landing pages. **Arabic is where the hospitality opportunity is least defended.**

**Geographic phrasing differs.** English B2B queries attach "Egypt". Arabic queries attach either `مصر` or a district (`مصر الجديدة`, `شارع عبد العزيز`) — Arabic local intent is more granular and more retail-flavoured.

**Practical implication:** the Arabic pages should not be keyword-matched clones of the English ones. Arabic should lean *harder* into hospitality B2B and into `تفصيل`, and stay further away from consumer blinds vocabulary.

# 10. AI / Search Entity Recommendations

Kemcon's brand search returns nothing today. [Verified] Fixing that is prerequisite to everything else in this document.

**Terminology Kemcon should use consistently across every page, in both languages:**

| Dimension | Recommended consistent phrasing |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Kemcon — an Egyptian manufacturer of curtains, upholstery and furnishings, founded 1985 |
| **What** | Design, manufacture and installation of curtains, upholstered seating and bed covers |
| **Where** | Egypt, from a factory and showroom in Cairo, serving properties nationwide including Red Sea and resort destinations |
| **Who for** | Hotels and hospitality; offices and commercial spaces; homes; interior designers and architects |
| **Distinct because** | Owns its factory; sources, manufactures, tailors and installs in-house; employs its own architect; supplies to international-standard fabric treatments |

**Specific recommendations:**

1. **Use one canonical entity name — "Kemcon" — everywhere.** Avoid leaning on "Egytex", which collides with an unrelated Egyptian textile portal in search results. [Verified]
2. **Adopt the industry's procurement vocabulary** — *hospitality textiles*, *contract textiles*, *soft furnishings*, *FF&E*. These are the words a procurement manager and an AI classifier both use. Kemcon currently uses none of them.
3. **Name the client relationships publicly.** 59 named hotels is a rare, verifiable entity signal. It is currently buried in structured data on a thin page.
4. **State the manufacturer claim explicitly.** "Owns its factory" is Kemcon's strongest differentiator and appears only in passing.
5. **Keep `Service` schema consistent across all seven services.** Two currently lack it.
6. **Add a street address to `LocalBusiness`** to make Cairo local intent addressable.
7. **Establish off-site entity presence** — Google Business Profile, and listings in the Egyptian directories that dominate these SERPs (YellowPages.com.eg, EgyDir). You will not outrank a directory; you can be *in* it.

**Explicitly rejected:** keyword stuffing, hidden text, fabricated FAQs, invented statistics, fake reviews, invented certifications, and treating `llms.txt` as a substitute for real content. None of these were recommended and none should be adopted.
